# Market research agent: your market, competitors and customers, with sources

Our AI market research agent gathers what is publicly known about your market, your competitors and your customers, and summarizes it with a source for every finding, so strategy starts from evidence instead of guesses.

Our market research agent researches your market, competitors, customer segments and benchmark metrics, the same inputs as the industry part of our audit, and lists the source of every finding. A person at t&f checks the sources and the conclusions before they shape a strategy.

## What the agent researches

### Market and category

How your category is searched, talked about and bought.

- What customers search for and ask
- Trends and seasonality in the category
- Where the demand sits: channels and regions

### Competitors

Who you are up against and how they present themselves.

- Positioning, offers and pricing pages
- Ads they run in public ad libraries
- Their content, reviews and gaps

### Customer segments

Who buys, why, and what stops them.

- Segments and the problems each one has
- Questions and objections from reviews and comments
- The words customers use, for ads and pages

### Benchmark metrics

Comparison points for your own numbers.

- Published benchmarks, each with its source
- Your own data next to them, where you share it
- Marked as an estimate where no source exists

- Every finding lists where it came from, so you can open the source yourself.
- Estimates are labelled as estimates.
- A person at t&f reads the summary, checks the sources and decides what goes into your strategy.
- Research gives the hypotheses; tests with your real customers decide.

## Questions about the market research agent

### Where does the market research agent get its information?

From public sources such as search results, competitors' websites, public ad libraries, reviews, social comments and published reports, plus any sales or CRM data you share. Each finding lists its source.

### Can AI market research replace talking to customers?

No. It narrows down what to ask and what to test. What customers actually do, in tests and in real sales data, is what decides the strategy.
